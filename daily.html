01 · DAILY LIFE & MANNERS

Daily Life & Manners

Small habits that make life in Japan easier. Learn them once and you will feel much more at ease.

ON THIS PAGE

01

Greetings and Basic Etiquette

Say “Ohayou gozaimasu” in the morning, “Konnichiwa” in the daytime, and “Konbanwa” in the evening. A small nod of the head (a light bow) is enough.

Take off your shoes at the entrance (genkan) of a home, and in some restaurants, temples, and schools.

Tip: Look for a raised floor or a shoe rack. That usually means “shoes off”.

02

Garbage Separation

Japan has very few public trash cans, and garbage is separated carefully. Put garbage out only on the correct day, usually in the morning, in the designated bag.

Tip: Ask your city office or landlord for the garbage calendar. Take your trash home if you cannot find a bin.

Five types of garbage

Burnable

Kitchen waste, paper scraps, small wood items

Non-burnable

Ceramics, glass items, small metal items

Plastic

Food trays, bottle caps, packaging with the プラ mark

Recyclables

Cans, glass bottles, PET bottles (remove labels)

Oversized

Furniture, bicycles. Book a pickup and pay a fee.

* Rules are different in each city. Always check your local garbage guide.

03

Quiet Behavior in Public

In trains, buses, and elevators, people keep their voices low. Set your phone to silent (manner mode) and avoid phone calls.

Tip: Short, quiet talking with friends is fine. Phone calls are the main thing to avoid.

04

Eating and Drinking Manners

Say “Itadakimasu” before you eat and “Gochisousama deshita” after you finish. These show thanks for the food.

Do not stand chopsticks upright in rice, and do not pass food from chopsticks to chopsticks. Slurping noodles is normal.

Tip: There is no tipping in Japan. Eating while walking is usually seen as impolite.

05

Onsen and Public Bath Rules

  1. Wash your body well before you enter the bath.
  2. Enter without clothes, a swimsuit, or slippers.
  3. Keep your towel and long hair out of the water.
  4. Watch your children and do not run or swim.
  5. Do not bring food or drinks into the bath.

Tip: Many baths do not allow tattoos or people who have been drinking. Check the rules before you go.
