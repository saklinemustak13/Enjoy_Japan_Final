Communication & Culture

START HERE

Three words to learn first

These three words are used every day and will help you a lot.

一

すみません

Sumimasen

EN · Excuse me / Sorry বাংলা · মাফ করবেন / দুঃখিত

The most useful word in Japan. Use it to call a waiter, to get past someone, to ask a question, or when you make a small mistake.

Example: “Sumimasen, eki wa doko desu ka?” (Excuse me, where is the station?)

二

ありがとう

Arigatou

EN · Thank you বাংলা · ধন্যবাদ

With friends, “Arigatou” is fine. With shop staff, teachers, or people you do not know, say “Arigatou gozaimasu”.

Example: “Arigatou gozaimasu.” (Thank you very much.)

三

お願いします

Onegaishimasu

EN · Please বাংলা · অনুগ্রহ করে

Use it when you order, ask for help, or hand something to someone.

Example: “Kore o onegaishimasu.” (This one, please.)

CUSTOMS

Polite behavior and customs

A few habits that will help you feel at home.

Click the image to see it larger
01

Waiting in Line and Personal Space

People wait in line at stations, shops, and restaurants. Stand behind the last person and do not skip ahead. People usually keep some distance and do not hug or touch strangers. A bow or a nod is a normal greeting.

Click the image to see it larger
02

At Shops and Restaurants

Staff greet you with “Irasshaimase” (Welcome). You do not need to answer, but a small nod is nice. Put money and cards on the small tray at the register.

Click the image to see it larger
03

Customs Foreigners Should Understand

  • “Chotto...” (a little...) is often a soft way to say no.
  • Business cards (meishi) are given and received with both hands.
  • Small gifts (omiyage) are a common way to say thanks.
  • Being on time is very important.

Tip: Smile, be patient, and say “Sumimasen”. It works almost everywhere.

CHECK YOURSELF

Quick quiz

Think of the answer, then click the question to see it.

Q1. You want to call a waiter. What do you say?

“Sumimasen!” You can also press the call button on the table.

Q2. Should you leave a tip at a restaurant?

No. Tipping is not a custom in Japan. A simple “Gochisousama deshita” is enough.

Q3. When do you wash your body at an onsen?

Before you enter the bath, at the washing area.

Q4. Which side do people usually stand on escalators in Tokyo?

The left side. But for safety, follow the signs and hold the handrail.
